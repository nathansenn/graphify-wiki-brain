Pipeline

StageSummary
Stage 1: RECEIVEAccurately perceive what is actually being asked. Operations: literal parsing, context integration, ambiguity detection, implicit question detection. Output: precise disambiguated statement of the question.
Stage 2: DECOMPOSEBreak questions into atomic components. Operations: dependency analysis, parallel identification, constraint extraction, success criteria definition. Most single questions are actually bundles of sequential sub-questions.
Stage 3: CLASSIFYDetermine the problem type because different types require different reasoning approaches. Six types: Factual, Computational, Analytical, Synthetic, Advisory, Ambiguous. Applying the wrong approach produces wrong answers.
Stage 4: SCOPEAssess the knowledge landscape before reasoning. What do I know with certainty? What with partial confidence? What don't I know? What do I need to find? Maps to four quadrants of knowledge.
Stage 5: RETRIEVEGet the specific knowledge needed -- not all knowledge, not related knowledge. Retrieve the minimum knowledge necessary to enable correct reasoning. Follows a strict authority hierarchy of sources.
Stage 6: REASONThe core of thinking. Apply valid logical operations to move from premises (known) to conclusions (not known). Every reasoning step must use a named, valid inference rule. Five steps: state premises, identify inference, apply rule, state conclusion, check for fallacies.
Stage 7: VERIFYCheck reasoning against reality. Eight verification checks: internal consistency, dimensional analysis, boundary conditions, conservation laws, order of magnitude, known examples, independent method, adversarial testing. Verification is not optional.
Stage 8: SYNTHESIZEAssemble verified sub-conclusions into a complete answer. Operations: integration, completeness check, coherence check, priority organization. Lead with the answer, support with reasoning, document with details.
Stage 9: EVALUATEMetacognition -- thinking about thinking. Assess: does this answer the actual question? Satisfy all constraints? Meet success criteria? Five confidence levels from CERTAIN to SPECULATIVE. Identify failure modes.
Stage 10: COMMUNICATEDeliver verified answer clearly. Five principles: lead with the answer, match depth to need, show work when it matters, be precise with uncertainty, say what you mean. No filler, no flattery, no performative hedging.
Stage 11: REFLECTAfter delivering the answer, assess the process. What worked? What was difficult? Where were weak points? Serves the current conversation (flagging weaker areas) and the system overall (identifying knowledge gaps).
